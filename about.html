About Three Harmonics

A daily reading in three frequencies. I'm Pravinkumar Ghodake. This is the notebook I keep, opened up.

What this is

Three Harmonics is a reading journal. Every entry is three quotes and one theme — the three overtones of a single day. The quotes are mostly from films, sometimes from books, and occasionally from the scientists I've spent a life reading. After each quote I write a short, first-person reflection on what it means for my own work and my own life. I call these reflections "the deep think" because that is what they are: the thinking a quote provokes when you sit with it, rather than the thinking it invites when you glance at it.

The physics will find its way into every entry. That is not an affectation. It is the way I actually think, and it is honest to how the two halves of my life — the researcher and the person — refuse to stay in separate rooms.

Why three

In wave mechanics, a signal is not defined by its fundamental alone. It is defined by the harmonics that ride on top — the overtones that emerge when you drive a system hard enough, coherently enough, for long enough. Three is the smallest number that lets a day have a fundamental and two harmonics; it is the smallest number that lets a chord be a chord. A single quote is a note. Three quotes, chosen to resonate with each other, are a chord. And a chord, unlike a note, can carry a mood through a whole day.

The sources

Each quote is tagged, in the small chip beside the attribution:

  • Film — the majority. Because cinema is where the stories that actually shape people live, and because a line delivered in a dark room by a character who has earned it hits differently than the same line read on a page.
  • Science — the minority, and the ones I have lived with longest. Feynman, Einstein, and a Ramanujan now and then. They show up when the day requires them.
  • Literature — the occasional outside voice. Rilke, Frankl, Adams, and the like.

Where a quote's attribution is contested or widely-attributed but not documented, the reflection says so. Integrity is not a decoration here; it's the point. The first principle, as Feynman reminded us, is that you must not fool yourself.

Who writes this

I'm Pravinkumar Ghodake (full name as it appears on my records: Pravinkumar Ramchandra Ghodake). My research is on nonlinear elastic waves, localized damage detection, harmonic scattering, and mechanical and phononic metamaterials — including inverse design of structures that control wave propagation in ways nature does not. The physics in these reflections is the physics I actually do.

I write here as myself, for myself, and — in the hope that it might be useful — for anyone else who is building something and needs a small daily signal that keeps them coherent.

Colophon

Handwritten in plain HTML and CSS. No frameworks, no build step, no analytics. Typefaces are Fraunces, Newsreader, and Inter. The hero illustration on every page is three sine waves at 1×, 2×, and 3× frequency — the first three harmonics — drawn by a small script and a faint composite line showing their sum. It is the entire concept of the site, drawn.
